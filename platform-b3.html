Platform overview

Write the study once. Every team builds from it.

Today, a protocol is a document that each later team re-keys: the casebook, the documents, the submission. ClinMaker keeps the protocol as one structured study and builds the rest from it, on open standards, with a record of every decision.

01

One structured study

The protocol is held as data on USDM 4.0, in ICH M11 order, so nothing downstream starts from a retyped copy.

02

Built on open standards

CDISC throughout: CDASH forms, controlled terminology, ODM and USDM exports you can take anywhere.

03

People decide

AI drafts, people approve. Reviews, approvals and e-signatures are recorded in an append-only audit trail.

04

Works with your EDC

The casebook exports to the EDC you already run. Your systems stay the source of truth.

Protocol workspace

From a document to a structured, approved protocol.

Five steps, in the product, on our demo study.

  1. 01

    Start from what you have

    Write a new protocol, or import one you already have as a PDF, Word file or USDM JSON. You review what was extracted, section by section, before anything is applied.

  2. 02

    Author in ICH M11 order

    A guided outline takes the team from study identification to regulatory. Sections tied to the study design update themselves when the design changes.

  3. 03

    Let AI draft, keep people in charge

    AI suggests objectives, endpoints, eligibility criteria and edits to the prose. Every suggestion is marked Advisory, and a person accepts or rejects it.

  4. 04

    Review, then approve once

    Each section moves from draft to in review to reviewed. When every comment is resolved and the completeness check passes, the protocol is approved with an e-signature.

  5. 05

    Export and amend

    Export the study as USDM 4.0 JSON, checked against CDISC rules, plus ClinicalTrials.gov and EU CTIS drafts and a PDF. When it changes, an amendment is assessed once, finding by finding.

Study Build

From the approved protocol to a casebook your EDC can load.

Five steps, from the same study.

  1. 01

    Start a build from the protocol

    Choose how to start: from the study definition, an uploaded protocol, a USDM file, or by hand. You confirm the EDC target and the CDASH and SDTM versions before the draft is created.

  2. 02

    Forms from the standards library

    Attach standard CDASH forms from the library. Each field carries its CDASH variable, and its SDTM target wherever the standard defines one, and you see the form as your EDC will show it.

  3. 03

    Lay out the schedule

    Place each form on the visits where it is collected. Edit checks are then generated by rules, not AI, and reviewed with the forms.

  4. 04

    Review, UAT and sign-off

    The build moves from draft to review, UAT, approved and live, with a snapshot at each step and a signed UAT approval.

  5. 05

    Export to your EDC

    Generate the file your EDC loads: Medidata Rave (ALS) or Veeva Vault (SDS), or CDISC ODM 1.3.2. The draft is checked before you download it, and a final export needs an approved build.

The platform

What both modules stand on.

One structured study

Stored in USDM 4.0, CDISC's model for study design. Every module reads from it and writes to it.

CDISC standards library

Controlled Terminology, CDASHIG and SDTMIG, built in.

Append-only audit trail

Hash-linked records, with a verifier that reports breaks and gaps. Database rules block edits and deletes.

E-signatures

The signer re-enters their password and gives a reason, and the signature is linked to the record it signs.

Roles and permissions

Fine-grained sponsor roles, separation of duties and blinding rules.

Single sign-on, MFA and SCIM

OIDC or SAML sign-in, multi-factor authentication and user provisioning.

Tenant isolation

Each customer's data kept apart at the database level.

AI governance

Model provider and routing settings, an AI evidence policy, and the Advisory label on every AI output.

Protocol library

Search and compare protocols, linked by therapeutic area and intervention, and reuse objectives, endpoints and eligibility.

Standards

Open CDISC standards you can export.

USDM 4.0 is CDISC's model for study design, ICH M11 the harmonized protocol template, CDASH the standard for collecting data and CDISC ODM an open format for study designs. Before the protocol is exported as USDM JSON, a gate runs CDISC CORE conformance checks, and overriding an error takes an e-signature and a reason.

Questions

ClinMaker and the systems you run today.

My EDC vendor already builds studies from the protocol. Why ClinMaker?

If you run one EDC and only need the build, your EDC vendor's tools may be enough. ClinMaker is for sponsors and CROs that want the protocol and the casebook on one structured study, run more than one EDC, or need to handle amendments once, before they reach the casebook.

ClinMaker isn't tied to one EDC. The casebook exports as CDISC ODM 1.3.2 XML, plus EDC-specific loader files [to confirm: which EDC loaders to name]. Your EDC stays the system your sites use and the source of the clinical data.

You're a new company with no customers yet. Why take the risk?

Don't bet a live study on us. Start with a 30-minute live demo. As an early adopter, you then run it alongside a study you've already built, so nothing live depends on it.

Why not have our CRO build the casebook?

Your CRO can keep doing the work, in ClinMaker. The casebook is built from the approved protocol instead of being re-keyed from a document, and an amendment is assessed once and carried into a new draft of the build. Your CRO works in the same study as you.

Can we use Protocol Digitization without Study Build?

Yes. Each module runs on the same platform. Study Build can also start from an uploaded protocol or a USDM JSON file. The structured study is yours and exports as USDM JSON.

Is ClinMaker validated?

Not yet. The platform is designed for 21 CFR Part 11, EU Annex 11 and ICH E6(R3) controls, but it has not been validated and no IQ/OQ/PQ has been executed. Validation documentation is being prepared; we share where it stands with every early adopter.

The platform holds no real patient data. See Trust & compliance for the detail.
