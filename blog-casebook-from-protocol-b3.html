Building the casebook from the protocol, not a PDF

Most casebooks are still built by hand from a specification written from the protocol. Here is what changes when the build starts from the study itself.

By the ClinMaker founders

In most studies, the database build starts with a document about a document. Someone reads the approved protocol, writes a build specification, and the EDC team builds the casebook from that specification. Each step is a chance for the protocol and the casebook to drift apart.

Start from the approved study

When the protocol is held as a structured study, the build can start from it directly. The visits and the schedule come from the study. The protocol version, and the CDASH and SDTM versions the build follows, are pinned to the build, so everyone knows exactly what it was built from.

Design the downstream in from the first form

Forms come from a CDASH library, and every field carries its CDASH variable and its SDTM target. The mapping to submission datasets is designed in from the first form, instead of being worked out after the data arrives.

Keep up when the protocol changes

A build linked to the study knows when the protocol has moved on, and shows that it is out of date. An amendment opens a new draft of the build, and the approved one stays as it is until the new one is approved.

Hand it to the EDC you run

The finished casebook is validated before export, then exported as Medidata Rave ALS, Veeva Vault SDS or CDISC ODM, so it loads into the EDC your sites already use.
