What changes for your team

Less re-keying for data management. Cleaner data downstream.

The build stops being a hand-made copy of the protocol and becomes part of the same study, designed to the standards your submission needs.

  1. Start from the protocol

    No more building from a PDF spec.

    Create the build from the approved, structured study. The protocol, CDASH and SDTM versions are pinned to the build, so everyone knows exactly what it was built from.

    • Built from the structured study
    • Protocol, CDASH and SDTM versions pinned
    • Or start a manual build when you need one

  2. Standards built in

    Standard forms, with SDTM targets already mapped.

    Pick forms from a CDASH library. Every field carries its CDASH variable and SDTM target, so the downstream mapping is designed in from the first form, not worked out after the data arrives.

    • CDASH form library
    • SDTM variable and dataset on every field
    • Lineage from each field back to its source

  3. Schedule and sign-off

    The schedule and the approvals, in one place.

    Place forms on the visits in the schedule of activities, then take each form through review and approval, with a record of every decision.

    • Forms placed on visits
    • Form-level review and approval
    • History on every change

  4. Protocol changes

    When the protocol changes, the build knows.

    A build linked to the study flags itself as out of date when the protocol moves on. An amendment opens a new draft of the build, the approved one stays as it is, and every build is validated before export.

    • Out-of-date flag against the protocol
    • A new draft per amendment
    • Validated before export to Rave, Vault or ODM

Why it matters

A build from a specification, compared with a build from the study.

The EDC at the end is the same. What changes is how much is retyped on the way, and how much survives the next amendment.
